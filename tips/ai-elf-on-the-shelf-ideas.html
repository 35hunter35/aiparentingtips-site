How can AI help me plan Elf on the Shelf ideas?

By Adam Hunter · October 24, 2026 · 3-minute read

Short answer: Tell a free AI chatbot your kids' ages, what you have around the house, and how many minutes you've got each night, and ask for a 24-night calendar of quick, kind setups. Then ask it for short elf notes to leave out. The prompt keeps it safe and gentle: nothing scary or messy, nothing near the stove, and no threats about the naughty list.

Prompt 1: the 24-night plan (paste into ChatGPT, Claude, Grok, or Gemini) 
Plan 24 nights of Elf on the Shelf ideas for my kids, ages [5 and 8].
Things we already have: [LEGO, toilet paper, mini marshmallows, paper, markers, stuffed animals, a toy car].
Time I have most nights: [5 minutes]. Busy nights when it must take under 2 minutes: [Dec 3, 10, 17].
Rules:
- Use only things we already have. No buying anything.
- Keep it kind and funny. Nothing scary, creepy, or gross, and no "elf in trouble or hurt" scenes.
- No threats, no punishments, and nothing about the naughty list or "reporting" bad behavior.
- Safe: no candles, matches, knives, medicine, cleaning products, or anything near the stove. Nothing up high that a kid might climb to reach.
- Low mess: no flour, glitter, paint, or permanent markers.
- No small loose pieces if a child is under 3. No chocolate, grapes, or raisins left where pets can reach.
- Make 4 of the nights "kindness" nights, where the elf suggests something nice to do for someone else.
Give me a table: Night | Setup (one sentence) | What you need | Minutes.
Prompt 2: short elf notes 
Write [24] short notes from our elf, [elf name], one for each night of this plan: [paste the plan or say "the plan above"].
- 2 or 3 sentences each, easy for a [age]-year-old to read.
- Warm, silly, and encouraging.
- Never mention watching the kids, reporting to Santa, being naughty, or losing presents.
- Don't use my kids' real names. Use "my friends" instead.
Prompt 3: I forgot, help! 
It's late and I forgot to move the elf. Give me 3 Elf on the Shelf ideas that take under 2 minutes, use things found in any kitchen or living room, and are safe and mess-free. Add one short elf note explaining why the elf was "resting" yesterday.

How to do it

  1. Paste Prompt 1 and fill in your kids' ages, your supplies, and your busiest nights.
  2. Read the whole list once and cross out anything that doesn't fit your house, your pets, or your kids.
  3. Put each night's idea in your phone calendar as a 9 p.m. reminder. Our family schedule prompt can merge it with everything else in December.
  4. Use Prompt 2 for the notes. They're tiny stories, a lot like our bedtime story prompt.
  5. Forgot a night? Paste Prompt 3 for a 2-minute save.

Ideas to skip, even if the AI suggests them

  • Anything with heat or sharp things: candles, lighters, the stove, the toaster, knives, scissors left out.
  • Medicine cabinet or cleaning products: even as a joke, it teaches kids those are play things.
  • Scary or "creepy" scenes: an elf that's hurt, trapped, or punished, or "evil elf" setups. Some kids find these upsetting at bedtime.
  • Surveillance and threats: notes saying the elf is watching or will tell Santa. They can turn a fun tradition into a stressful one.
  • Choking hazards for little ones: small items, mini marshmallows, button batteries, or magnets anywhere a child under 3 can reach.
  • Pet hazards: chocolate, grapes, raisins, or anything sweetened with xylitol left where the dog can reach it.
  • AI photos of your kids with the elf: skip uploading their photos. An elf coloring page made from a description is a fun swap.

Safety first: Don't type your kids' full names, school, address, or photos into any AI tool. It doesn't need them. Most AI chatbots are for ages 13 and up (ChatGPT and Grok also need a parent's permission under 18), and Claude is 18+, so check each tool's age rules before a child uses it.

FAQ

Can ChatGPT really plan Elf on the Shelf ideas?

Yes. Give it your kids' ages, your supplies, and your time, and it'll draft a full 24-night plan in a minute or two. The rules in Prompt 1 matter, because without them AI ideas tend to be vague or need things you don't have.

What do I do if I forget to move the elf?

Paste Prompt 3. You'll get three 2-minute ideas plus a note explaining the elf was resting. It happens to everyone.

Should I make AI pictures of my kids with the elf?

We'd skip it. Those trends ask you to upload your child's photo. Make an elf coloring page from a description instead, or take a real photo yourself.

How do I keep the elf cheap?

The prompt says "use only things we already have," and you can add "no food" if you like. Toilet paper, LEGO, paper, and stuffed animals cover most nights.

Is it okay to use the elf to get kids to behave?

Every family decides that for themselves. The prompts here keep the elf kind and leave out threats, which many parents find makes December calmer. Kindness nights are a gentle way to encourage good behavior.
