How can I use AI to make a meal plan for a picky eater?

By Adam Hunter · October 17, 2026 · 3-minute read

Short answer: Tell ChatGPT, Claude, Grok, or Gemini the handful of foods your picky eater already likes, and ask it to build a week of family dinners around those "safe" foods. The prompt below gets you five dinners, a small new food to try next to each one, and a grocery list, all in about five minutes.

Copy-paste prompt (works in free ChatGPT, Claude, Grok, and Gemini) 
You are a patient family meal planner who helps parents of picky eaters.
Plan 5 weeknight dinners for a family of [number] with a picky eater aged [age].
Foods my picky eater reliably eats: [list 5-8 foods].
Hard limits (allergies or foods we never serve): [list, or "none"].
Rules:
- Every dinner includes at least one food from the "reliably eats" list.
- The rest of the family gets a normal meal, so I only cook once (no separate kid meal).
- Next to each dinner, suggest one tiny "try a bite" food that is similar to something they already like.
- Each dinner takes 30 minutes or less.
Then give me one grocery list grouped by store section, with quantities.

How to do it

  1. Open any free AI chatbot: ChatGPT, Claude, Grok, or Gemini.
  2. Copy the prompt and fill in the [brackets] with your kid's safe foods and any allergies.
  3. Read the plan. Reply with swaps like "no fish" or "make Thursday a sheet-pan meal."
  4. Ask for the grocery list grouped by store section, then screenshot it.

Double-check: AI can get allergens wrong. Always read labels yourself, and talk to your pediatrician if picky eating is affecting growth or energy.

Safety first: Don't type your kids' full names, school, address, or photos into any AI tool. It doesn't need them. Most AI chatbots require users to be at least 13 (with a parent's permission under 18), and Claude requires users to be 18 or older, so check each tool's age rules before a child uses it.

FAQ

Do I need the paid version of ChatGPT?

No. This prompt works in the free versions of ChatGPT, Claude, Grok, and Gemini.

Will this make my picky eater eat vegetables?

No prompt can promise that. The idea is to keep a safe food on every plate and put one small new food nearby, so trying it feels low-pressure.

Can it handle allergies?

You can list allergies as hard limits and the AI will plan around them, but it can still make mistakes. Always check ingredient labels yourself.

What if my kid only eats five foods?

That's fine. List those five, and ask the AI to keep every dinner close to them. You can widen the list a little each week.
