How can I use AI to make a meal plan for a picky eater?

By Adam Hunter · October 17, 2026 · 3-minute read

Short answer: Tell the AI your kids' ages, what they won't eat, any allergies, your budget, and how many dinners you need, and ask for a simple plan in a table. Then ask it to turn that plan into a grocery list sorted by store section. It takes about 5 minutes. It's been tested in the free versions of ChatGPT and Gemini.

Copy-paste prompt (tested in free ChatGPT and Gemini) 
Act as a practical family meal planner. Plan [5] weeknight dinners for my family.

About us:
- Kids' ages: [6 and 9]
- Picky-eater rules: [one kid won't eat anything green; no spicy food]
- Allergies or foods we avoid: [none / list them]
- Budget for these dinners: about [$150] for the week
- Time I have to cook on a weeknight: [30 minutes or less]
- Already in my pantry/fridge: [rice, pasta, eggs, frozen chicken, canned tomatoes]
- Equipment: [oven, stovetop, slow cooker]

Rules:
- Use regular grocery-store ingredients. No hard-to-find items.
- Use what I already have before adding new items.
- Reuse ingredients across meals so less goes to waste.
- Don't repeat the same kind of dinner more than twice.
- For picky eaters, keep the "no" food separate so it's easy to leave off
  the plate. Don't hide it.
- Repeat my allergies back to me at the top so I can check you got them right.
- Flag any ingredient that often hides my allergens (breads, tortillas, sauces, broths, soy sauce, and seasoning mixes).

Give me:
1. A table with columns: Night | Dinner | Prep + cook time | Picky-eater tip
2. A short note on any leftovers I can reuse the next night.
Keep it simple. No recipes yet. I'll ask for any recipe I want.
Only give me the allergy check, the table, and the leftovers note. Nothing else.

How to do it

  1. Open your AI app. ChatGPT or Gemini, free version, no sign-in needed. Claude and Grok should work too.
  2. Copy the prompt and paste it into a new chat.
  3. Fill in the [brackets] with your family's details. Use ages, not names.
  4. Send it and check the allergy line at the top. Don't like a meal? Reply "Swap Wednesday for something different that uses mostly the same ingredients. Keep all my rules."
  5. Ask for the grocery list: "Now make a grocery list for this plan. Leave out anything I said I already have. Sort it by store section, show quantities, and give one rough total at the end." The total is a ballpark, so treat it as a rough guide.

Double-check: AI can get allergens wrong, and its grocery totals can be off or even contradict themselves. Always read labels yourself, and check with your doctor or dietitian about allergies or special diets.

Safety first: Don't type your kids' full names, school, address, or photos into any AI tool. It doesn't need them. Most AI chatbots are for ages 13 and up (ChatGPT and Grok also need a parent's permission under 18), and Claude is 18+, so check each tool's age rules before a child uses it.

FAQ

Does this work in Gemini, Grok, or Claude, or just ChatGPT?

This prompt was tested in the free versions of ChatGPT and Gemini. ChatGPT followed it closely. Free Gemini got the basics right but slipped on a couple of rules (it didn't flag soy sauce as a possible allergen in a stir-fry, planned three pasta nights after a swap, and its grocery total was far too low), so give its plan a quick read and ask it to fix anything off. If your family has allergies, don't rely on the AI's allergen flags. Read every label yourself. Claude and Grok should handle it too, but this exact prompt hasn't been run there yet. Whatever app you use, check the allergy line at the top of the answer. If you get a messy answer, ask it to "put that in a table."

Do I need the paid version?

No. The free versions of ChatGPT and Gemini both worked, without even signing in. Paid versions can be faster or give longer answers, but you don't need one for a weekly meal plan.

Can I trust it with food allergies?

Use it as a starting point, not the final word. AI can make mistakes, including about ingredients. Always read the labels yourself, and check with your doctor or dietitian about allergies or special diets. That's why the prompt asks the AI to repeat your allergies back to you.

Is it safe to share info about my kids?

Share only what the AI needs: ages and food preferences. Skip names, schools, photos, and health records. "My 6-year-old won't eat green food" gets you the same plan.
