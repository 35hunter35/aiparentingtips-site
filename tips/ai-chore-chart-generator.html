How can I make a chore chart with AI for free?

By Adam Hunter · October 17, 2026 · 3-minute read

Short answer: Tell a free AI chatbot your kids' ages and how much time they have each day, and the prompt below builds a fair weekly chore chart as a table you can print. It picks age-appropriate jobs, rotates the ones nobody likes, and can add points or allowance if you want.

Copy-paste prompt (works in free ChatGPT, Claude, Grok, and Gemini) 
Make a weekly chore chart for my kids.
Kids (by initial): [e.g., "K, age 9" and "M, age 5"].
Time each kid can spend on chores: about [10-15] minutes on school days, [20-30] on weekends.
Rules:
- Only age-appropriate chores, with a one-line "how to" for each so a kid can do it alone.
- Split the work fairly by age, and rotate the least-liked chores each week.
- Show it as a table: days across the top, kids down the side.
- Add an optional points column. [Include allowance: yes/no. If yes, budget is $[amount] per week.]
- Keep the wording short and friendly so kids can read it.

How to do it

  1. Open ChatGPT, Claude, Grok, or Gemini (free is fine).
  2. Paste the prompt and fill in ages and minutes per day. Use first initials, not full names.
  3. Ask for changes like "no chores on Wednesday, we have practice."
  4. Copy the table into Google Docs or Word, then print it for the fridge.

Parent check: You know your kids best. Swap anything that involves sharp knives, hot stoves, or chemicals for something safer for their age.

Safety first: Don't type your kids' full names, school, address, or photos into any AI tool. It doesn't need them. Most AI chatbots require users to be at least 13 (with a parent's permission under 18), and Claude requires users to be 18 or older, so check each tool's age rules before a child uses it.

FAQ

Is there a free AI chore chart generator?

Yes. Any free AI chatbot can make a chore chart with this prompt. You get a table you can paste into a document and print.

What chores are right for a 5-year-old?

Common picks are making the bed, putting toys away, feeding a pet, and matching socks. Ask the AI to suggest more for your kid's age, then use your judgment.

Should chores be tied to allowance?

Families do it both ways. The prompt lets you choose, and you can ask the AI to split it into everyday "family" chores and extra paid jobs.

How do I stop the fights over who does what?

Ask the AI to rotate the least-liked jobs weekly and print the chart, so the rules live on the fridge instead of in an argument.
