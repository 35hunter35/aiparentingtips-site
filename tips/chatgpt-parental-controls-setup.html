How do I set up ChatGPT parental controls?

By Adam Hunter · October 24, 2026 · 4-minute read

Short answer: On chatgpt.com, open Settings, choose Parental controls, then Add family member, and send your teen an invite by email or text. Once they accept, you can set quiet hours, start new chats in study mode, and switch off features like voice mode or image generation. Parental controls don't let you read your teen's chats, so they work best alongside a real conversation about how your family uses AI.

Prompt 1: plan the talk with your teen first (paste into ChatGPT, Claude, Grok, or Gemini) 
Help me plan a calm, 10-minute talk with my [age]-year-old about linking our ChatGPT accounts with parental controls.
What I want to set up: [quiet hours on school nights / study mode for homework / no image generation].
What my teen mostly uses ChatGPT for: [homework / ideas / questions].
Please give me:
1. A friendly opening line that doesn't sound like I don't trust them.
2. Three honest points to explain, including that I can't read their chats.
3. Two questions to ask them, so they help choose the settings.
4. A short, kind answer if they say "So you're spying on me?"
Keep it warm and simple. No lectures.
Prompt 2: after you're linked, ask ChatGPT to explain your teen's settings (paste into your own ChatGPT) 
Show me the parental control settings for my linked teen.
For each one, tell me in one plain sentence what it does and whether it's on or off right now.
Don't change anything yet. I'll tell you what I want to change.

How to do it

  1. Sign in to your own ChatGPT account on the web at chatgpt.com. A free account is fine. OpenAI's steps for sending the invite are written for the web.
  2. Open Settings from your profile menu, select Parental controls, then Add family member.
  3. Pick Use phone or Use email, enter your teen's number or email, and select Send. Your teen can also invite you from their own Settings.
  4. Your teen accepts from the text or email link. If the link opens ChatGPT without an Accept button, they sign in on the web, go to Settings > Parental controls, and accept the pending invite there.
  5. Back in Settings > Parental controls, select your teen under Family members and choose their settings. The list below explains each one.
  6. Under Safety notifications, select Manage notifications and check that the email or phone number is one you actually look at.

What you can change

These are the main settings OpenAI lists for a linked teen account, with their defaults:

  • Quiet hours: times your teen can't use ChatGPT at all, like after 9 p.m. on school nights. Off until you set it. You can add more than one block.
  • Study mode and Study hours: new chats can start in study mode, which gives hints and questions instead of answers. This doesn't block anything, and it's off by default. Common Sense Media found teens can easily turn it off. Pair it with our homework tutor prompt.
  • Reduce sensitive content: on by default. Adds filters for things like graphic content, viral challenges, and romantic or violent roleplay.
  • Voice Mode and Image generation: both on by default. Turn either off if you'd rather they not be used.
  • Reference saved memories: whether ChatGPT remembers details across chats. On by default in most places.
  • Improve the model for everyone: on by default. Turn it off if you don't want your teen's chats used to train OpenAI's models.
  • Health: on by default where it's offered. Controls whether your teen can use ChatGPT's Health feature and connect health data. It doesn't stop general health questions.

Some changes only apply to new chats, so ask your teen to start a fresh chat after you change something.

What parental controls don't do

  • You can't read their chats. Linking doesn't show you your teen's conversations, chat history, or what they're doing in real time.
  • Safety alerts are limited. OpenAI may notify you if trained reviewers spot a serious self-harm concern or signs of disordered eating, or if the account is deactivated over violence. Alerts don't include the conversation, they can take a few hours to start working after you link, and OpenAI says they won't catch everything.
  • Your teen can unlink. Either of you can unlink at any time. If your teen does, you get a notification.
  • One parent per teen. A teen can be linked to only one parent or guardian at a time. One parent can link several teens.
  • It ends at 18. When ChatGPT identifies the account as 18 or older, the controls are removed and you're both notified.

Checked against OpenAI's Help Center on October 18, 2026: Managing parental controls in ChatGPT and ChatGPT for Teens. Settings change, so check there if something looks different.

Don't rely on alerts alone: In October 2026, Common Sense Media rated ChatGPT for Teens an "Unacceptable Risk", partly because parent alerts didn't fire in its tests. OpenAI disputes how the tests were run. Use the controls, and keep talking with your teen. If your child is in crisis, call or text 988 (US).

Safety first: Don't type your kids' full names, school, address, or photos into any AI tool. It doesn't need them. Most AI chatbots are for ages 13 and up (ChatGPT and Grok also need a parent's permission under 18), and Claude is 18+, so check each tool's age rules before a child uses it.

FAQ

Can I see my teen's ChatGPT chats?

No. OpenAI says parental controls don't give parents access to a teen's conversations, chat history, or real-time activity, and safety notifications don't include the chat either. If you want to know how they use it, ask them to show you. That works better than any setting.

Does my teen have to agree to link accounts?

Yes. Your teen has to accept the invite before anything is linked, and either of you can unlink later. That's why it helps to talk first. Prompt 1 above helps you plan that talk.

Can my kid under 13 use ChatGPT with parental controls?

No. OpenAI's rules say users must be 13 or older, with a parent's permission under 18. For younger kids, use ChatGPT together on your own account, and see our guide to AI age rules. A good first step is explaining what AI is.

Do I need a paid ChatGPT plan?

No. OpenAI says ChatGPT for Teens is available on Free and paid personal plans, and you can link from a free parent account.

Can my teen get around quiet hours?

Quiet hours only cover the linked ChatGPT account. A teen could still use another account, another device, or another AI app. Device-level tools like Apple Screen Time or Google Family Link cover more, but the family rules you agree on together matter most.
