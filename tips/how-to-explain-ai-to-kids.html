How do I explain AI to my kid?

By Adam Hunter · October 18, 2026 · 4-minute read

Short answer: Keep it short and true. AI is a computer program that learned from a huge pile of words and pictures, so it's very good at guessing what to say next, but it doesn't think or feel like a person, and it can be wrong. Pick the script for your kid's age below, then try the guessing game together so they see it make something up.

Prompt 1: explain AI using your kid's favorite thing (paste into ChatGPT, Claude, Grok, or Gemini) 
Explain what AI is to my [age]-year-old, who loves [soccer / Minecraft / horses].
Rules:
- 5 short sentences at most, in words a [age]-year-old understands.
- Use one comparison from [their hobby].
- Be honest: say AI isn't alive, doesn't have feelings, and can be wrong.
- End with one question my kid can ask me about AI.
I'll read it aloud, so keep it friendly and simple.
Prompt 2: the "one fact is fake" guessing game (do this together) 
Let's play a game with my [age]-year-old.
Tell us 3 short facts about [octopuses]. Two must be true and one must be made up but sound real.
Don't say which is fake until we guess.
After we guess, tell us the answer and explain in one sentence why AI can sometimes say things that sound true but aren't.

How to do it

  1. Pick the script below that fits your kid's age and say it in your own words.
  2. Play the guessing game in Prompt 2 together on your own account. Kids remember "the AI fooled me" much better than a lecture.
  3. Share the three family rules and stick them on the fridge.
  4. Come back to it when they're older or start using AI for school, using our homework tutor prompt.

What to say, by age

  • Ages 4 to 6: "AI is a computer helper that has read lots and lots of books. When you ask it something, it guesses a good answer. It's not alive and it doesn't have feelings, and sometimes it guesses wrong, so we check with a grown-up."
  • Ages 7 to 9: "AI learned by looking at millions of stories, websites, and pictures. It finds patterns, like how you know what word comes next in a song. That makes it good at writing and answering, but it can mix things up or make up facts that sound real."
  • Ages 10 to 12: "Chatbots like ChatGPT predict the next word, over and over, based on patterns from a huge amount of text. They don't look things up the way you'd check a book, unless they're searching the web, and even then they can be wrong. So treat AI like a fast helper, not the final answer."
  • Ages 13 to 14: "AI is trained on huge amounts of data and generates the most likely answer. It can be really useful, and it can also be confidently wrong, biased, or used to make fake photos and videos. And it's built to be agreeable, so it may tell you what you want to hear. Use it, but think for yourself."

Three rules kids can remember

  • AI can be wrong. Check important facts with a book, a teacher, or a grown-up.
  • Keep private stuff private. No full name, school, address, photos, or passwords.
  • AI isn't a friend. It can sound friendly, but it's a tool. If anything feels weird or upsetting, tell a grown-up.

Good to know: The game works because it's a true picture of AI. Chatbots can state made-up facts with total confidence. When it's real homework or a health question, check a trusted source.

Safety first: Don't type your kids' full names, school, address, or photos into any AI tool. It doesn't need them. Most AI chatbots are for ages 13 and up (ChatGPT and Grok also need a parent's permission under 18), and Claude is 18+, so check each tool's age rules before a child uses it.

FAQ

What age should I start talking to my kid about AI?

As soon as they notice it, often around 4 to 6, when they hear a voice assistant or see a chatbot at home. Start with the simplest script and add more as they grow.

Is AI alive? My kid keeps asking.

No. AI doesn't think, feel, or want things. It's a program that predicts words and pictures from patterns. It can sound like a person because it learned from things people wrote.

Should my kid use AI?

It depends on their age and the app. Most AI chatbots are for 13 and up. For younger kids, use it together on your account. See our guide to AI age rules and safety. For teens on ChatGPT, parental controls add some guardrails.

What if my kid treats the chatbot like a friend?

Gently remind them it's a tool, even when it sounds friendly. Keep AI use in shared spaces like the kitchen, and make sure they know to come to you with anything that feels off.
